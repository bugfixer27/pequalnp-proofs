L-meta-652. A computer-assisted lower bound for the three cyclic matchings

Status
This package gives a mathematical reduction to finite Boolean formulas and records independent LRAT verification of the resulting unsatisfiability certificates. Its Lean component currently establishes the upper bound, two structural restrictions, and a general projection lemma. A Lean proof connecting the complete SAT encoding to the posted statement and discharging its finite checks is still needed for submission.

Statement
Write the six inputs as a_i,b_j with i,j in Z/3. For k in Z/3, the required output f_k is the disjunction over i of a_i AND b_(i+k). Wires can be shared without cost. Each AND, OR or NOT gate costs one. Computing the nine distinct products and two disjunctions for each output costs fifteen gates. The argument below excludes a smaller circuit.

A minimum circuit
Take a smallest circuit computing the three outputs. Its gates can be taken to be AND and OR with two distinct earlier wires and NOT with one earlier wire. An unavailable wire in the project model reads false. Empty or unary gates, constant signals and repeated operands simplify by the usual Boolean identities. A gate computing a previous signal can be replaced everywhere by that signal. A gate unused by another gate or by an output can be removed. These operations preserve the chosen output functions. All three target outputs are nonconstant and differ from the input wires. When checking a restricted instance we can drop projected outputs that are constant, are inputs, or repeat another required output. This only weakens that lower-bound test.

Checking smaller restrictions
For each assignment to selected raw inputs, simulate the circuit on the remaining inputs. Any normalized gate with a constant operand can be removed, as the result either is constant or is an earlier signal. This includes NOT applied to a constant. Keep a map from the old wire to its constant or surviving wire so the substitution is also valid at later gates.

Fixing one raw input to zero leaves a problem requiring at least nine gates. Fixing one to one leaves a problem requiring at least twelve. To check the lower bounds, verify the two CNFs named 0xxxxx and 1xxxxx. Each describes a circuit with exactly one fewer gate than the claimed minimum. Dummy gates are allowed and no gate must be an output of the last slot, so a shorter circuit embeds in this formulation. The auxiliary bounds on restrictions of these instances are themselves checked by smaller CNFs without any auxiliary restriction assumptions. A minimum of zero adds no restriction. At minimum one, a zero-slot formula with a nonprimitive target literally contains the empty clause. The manifest marks these separately. The small cases are indexed by minima.json.

The symmetries used for these restrictions are the independent cyclic shifts on the two triples, simultaneous reversal of both triples, and interchange of the two triples. Each operation permutes the three outputs. In particular, any single raw input can be sent to any other. For smaller partially fixed inputs, signature in encoding.py returns the least cube in this orbit.

Consequences for a candidate with at most fourteen gates
1. Each input is read by at most two gates. Fix that input to one. Every direct reader is removed, and at least twelve gates must survive.
2. If both inputs of a binary gate are raw, each must also have another reader. Fix one input to the controlling value of this gate. The other remains essential to the output tuple. For example, varying a left input changes an output when the right triple is a suitable singleton. A required value for one other right coordinate can be respected by choosing that singleton accordingly. A required value in the left triple can coexist with this test. Interchange the sides for a right input.
3. If x has two readers, neither can be OR or NOT. Such a gate becomes constant at x=1. Its value cannot be a designated output because all three outputs on this cofactor are nonconstant. It therefore has a later consumer. If that consumer also reads x, the original pair reduces by absorption for an OR reader, or to a constant for a NOT reader. This contradicts minimality. Otherwise, fixing x to one removes the two direct readers and the additional consumer, leaving at most eleven gates.
4. In particular a raw binary gate must be AND. No two such gates can share a raw input x. If their partners are different, fixing both partners to zero blocks both paths out of x, although x still affects an output. To see this, choose a singleton on the opposite side consistent with the two prescribed zeros and set all other inputs on x's side to zero. If the partners coincide the two gates compute the same function. The accompanying Lean file proves the general two-pin sensitivity property and a gate-reader version of it.
5. Fewer than fourteen gates are impossible. If the first gate is binary, fixing either of its inputs to one removes its two readers. If the first gate is NOT, fixing the input to one removes it and a consumer of its constant output. The NOT cannot itself be a required output. Either way the twelve-gate cofactor lower bound implies an original size of at least fourteen.
6. In a minimum circuit, a binary gate cannot take both an earlier binary gate and one of that earlier gate's operands. The four AND/OR combinations on a signal and its conjunction or disjunction with another signal are aliases. This argument does not apply to an earlier NOT and the corresponding clause explicitly permits that case.

The three main cases
If there is a binary gate on two raw inputs, place one such gate first. It is an AND by steps 2 and 3. Either its inputs are in the same triple or they are in different triples. In the first case they can be relabelled as raw inputs 0 and 1. In the second they can be relabelled as 0 and 3. No output is fixed to the last gate when this symmetry is used. In the remaining case there are no binary gates on two raw inputs and the first gate is a NOT on an input that can be relabelled as 0. These are the instances same, cross and not. By step 5 it suffices to check size fourteen.

How the CNFs represent circuits
Every gate chooses exactly one operation and one wire for each port. Binary gates use distinct inputs with the larger index in the first port. A NOT repeats its one effective input in both port selectors. Each truth-table row has a variable for every computed wire. Implications selected by the port and operation literals impose the ordinary Boolean truth table. Output selectors enforce all desired output values on every tested row.

The gate order can be sorted lexicographically by the larger and then smaller operand index. If adjacent gates form an inversion, the second cannot use the first. Swapping them and renumbering the downstream references leaves a valid circuit and reduces the lexicographic sequence of pairs. This process terminates since there are finitely many indexed circuits at a fixed size. In the main cases the chosen first gate stays fixed and only the suffix is sorted. In the small cases dummy gates can be appended without changing the outputs and the complete sequence can then be sorted. For the main cases a gate in a minimum circuit must be used as a later input or an output. An output depending on d raw variables cannot occur before d-1 gates have run. Tracing its single output wire backwards across one two-input gate adds at most one distinct wire to the frontier. The cofactor verification files omit that output-position shortcut.

The sensitivity clauses are valid for any two rows differing only in x where a target changes. At least one direct reader of x must differ on those rows. Otherwise induction through the gates shows that every gate computes the same bit on both rows, including all designated output gates.

The cofactor clauses propagate forced zero and one marks along existing gates. Proper constants in the fixed inputs are marked, and the AND, OR and NOT clauses propagate the marks implied by their operands. A gate with a marked operand cannot count as surviving. If a projected circuit requires m gates, at least m gates can be left unmarked by this simplification. For any genuine satisfying topology the assignment using exactly the syntactically forced marks witnesses these clauses. They are used only with lower bounds already checked at smaller instances.

Reproduction
Python 3.9.6 and python-sat 1.9.dev24 were used to generate the CNFs. CaDiCaL with LRAT support produced certificates. The independent verifier was lrat-trim at commit 423194927df747528d5b7364806b5b77b2660c12 from https://github.com/arminbiere/lrat-trim. The 69 usage tests in the checker's build passed. The manifest records the original CNF hashes, the verdicts and the hashes of the trimmed binary proof files from this run. A different CaDiCaL release may produce a different valid proof of the same CNF.

In an environment with python-sat installed, the following regenerates the CNFs and verifies that every formula has exactly the recorded SHA-256 hash.
  python3 verify.py --generate-only --output certificates

With CaDiCaL and lrat-trim on the executable search path, the following also produces and independently checks fresh LRAT certificates.
  python3 verify.py --output certificates
The two executable paths can be passed explicitly with --cadical and --checker. Allow several GB of temporary disk and enough memory for the large five-input certificate. A 20 exit status is the UNSAT success status for both executables. The driver checks both the verifier exit status and its VERIFIED verdict. The expensive binary files can be regenerated from this small source package.

Formal gap
LRAT certifies unsatisfiability of a specific CNF. The mathematical argument above is what relates the checked CNFs to the unrestricted circuit statement. The project requires this last step, including the validity of all finite checks, within the trust model and size limit of its Lean submission. The package has not been submitted as a proof or credited to a contributor.

Compact Lean verification in progress
Search.lean proves unrestricted_eleven, excluding every circuit of size at most ten for the three posted outputs. It verifies that every first gate permits a row restriction that saves at least two gates in a minimum circuit. The four-input lower bounds for ****11 and **0**1 imply a nine-gate bound for the five-input positive cofactor. Applying the same argument to the six-input problem proves the unconditional eleven-gate minimum lower bound. In general, full_from_positive raises any lower bound for the positive cofactor by two gates. Lean's axiom report for unrestricted_eleven listed propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound.
SmallCases.lean checks all 22 positive auxiliary lower bounds on two-, three- and four-input restrictions. The external computation and written argument reach a fifteen-gate minimum. The unrestricted Lean proof still needs to exclude sizes eleven through fourteen.
